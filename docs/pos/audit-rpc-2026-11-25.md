# RPC and permission audit (2026-11-25)

Read-only review of every SQL function in `supabase/migrations` (69 functions; 45 are callable by any logged-in shop account), plus RLS, table grants, views, storage, and the LINE/cron API routes. Nothing was run against a database, so production drift is not ruled out.

The threat model: a staff member can call any RPC granted to `authenticated`, or query any exposed table or view, straight from browser devtools with their own session. UI checks therefore don't count.

Full per-area notes, including the per-table permission matrix, are outside the repo in the session notes folder: `rpc-audit-sales-stock.md`, `rpc-audit-cash.md`, `rpc-audit-rls-views.md`. The SF and consignment findings from 2026-11-24 are already fixed in PR #6 and are not repeated here.

## What is solid

- **No table is writable by staff or anon directly.** Every public table has RLS on, core tables have owner-only write policies, and anon is revoked everywhere. Cash-book tables have no grants at all.
- **Every RPC grant is correct.** Each one is revoked from `public`/`anon` and granted on its exact current signature, and old overloads were dropped. Every function uses `search_path=''`, and none uses dynamic SQL.
- **Roles can't be forged.** `pos_is_member()`/`pos_is_owner()` read `profiles`, which only the owner can write. Nothing trusts a role sent by the client.
- **The cash book is well guarded.**
  - Owner-only actions are gated.
  - Money is validated (unsigned, at most 2 decimals), and neither fund can go negative.
  - Committed operations and movements are immutable.
  - A replay must match exactly, every writer locks the book row, and legacy RPCs can't bypass it once the book is open.
- **The external routes are safe.**
  - The LINE webhook checks a timing-safe HMAC and calls no RPC.
  - The cron route is behind `CRON_SECRET`, fails closed, and keeps the service-role key on the server.

## Findings to fix

Confirmed by reading the code unless marked otherwise.

| # | Severity | Problem | Where | Fix direction |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | High (functional) | Once the cash book is open, a repair job **can never be marked abandoned**. The repair-collection trigger accepts a `closed_at` change only inside a `repair_close` cash operation, and `rpc_cash_execute` has no abandon action. Part costs of abandoned jobs then never reach repair profit (ADR 0011). | `rpc_set_repair_status` (20260810120000:61) vs `ucom_cash_guard_repair_collection` (20261007104638:68) | Let the trigger pass a change to `abandoned` while `sale_id` stays null. |
| 2 | Medium | **Staff choose the sale date freely.** `sold_at` comes from the payload with no limit. A past day, a future day or `infinity` moves the sale into another day's report and deposit allocation. The cash book then freezes that day, so even the owner can't fix it. | `rpc_create_sale` (20260816102841:133), reached via `rpc_cash_execute` sale / repair_close | Require `isfinite(sold_at)`, and for non-owners limit it to the offline window (for example opened_at or now−48h, up to now+5 min). |
| 3 | Medium | **`'NaN'` passes every `>= 0` check.** Postgres stores NaN in `numeric(12,2)` and ranks it above every number. A NaN stock cost, part cost or commission makes the owner's profit or commission totals NaN. *Inferred from Postgres semantics; not tested.* | `rpc_set_stock_cost`, `rpc_upsert_product`, `rpc_set_part_cost`, `rpc_record_sf_commission`; also `rpc_create_sale`/top-up guard before the cash book opens | Use one shared money check (as `ucom_cash_money` does), or add `CHECK (x <> 'NaN')` on the money columns. |
| 4 | Medium | **A repair-close bill can carry product, device or top-up lines.** Their revenue and cost drop out of the report, because repair profit is computed as revenue minus part cost only. Needs a direct RPC call. | `rpc_close_repair_job` (20260805120200:274), `v_report_entries` repair section | Reject non-service lines in a repair close. |
| 5 | Medium | **Saving a product overwrites qty with a stale value.** If another tab sold units after the form was opened, saving the edit resurrects them. A missing qty or price key becomes 0. | `rpc_upsert_product` (20260929094124:62) | Keep current values for missing keys, and check the expected old qty (or adjust by a delta). |
| 6 | Medium (policy) | **Staff can rebuild most of the P&L.** `v_pos_stock` returns the cost of sold and financed devices, and the close-day views return all history (bills, items, expenses, part costs). ADR 0021 allows cost only while a record is open, and no profit. | `v_pos_stock` (20260929094124:9), `v_close_day_*` views | For non-owners, show cost only on open records and limit close-day views to the current or open cash days. Owner to confirm. |

## Low priority

- **Sales and repairs (`rpc_create_sale`, `rpc_set_part_cost`, `rpc_delete_sf_order`, `rpc_create_repair_job`):**
  - A line discount can exceed price × qty.
  - An inactive product can still be sold.
  - A part cost can be changed on an abandoned job.
  - `rpc_delete_sf_order` has no row lock, so a device financed at the same moment can be deleted.
  - `quoted_price` is not validated.
- **Cash book:**
  - The receiving-device token is stored in plaintext in operation payloads.
  - Staff can relabel pending cash as drawer cash through handover + return, with no owner review.
  - A closing made after Bangkok midnight lands on the next date.
  - Status reads take `FOR UPDATE` on the book row and queue behind writers.
  - The allocation assert scans the whole history on every operation.
  - Pre-opening expense and income RPCs have weak validation.
  - The reminder RPCs' service-role check passes when the role is NULL. The grants still restrict them.
- **Permissions:**
  - Owner sessions can write tables directly and bypass RPC rules (`*_owner_all FOR ALL`).
  - Leftover grants on `v_sale_profit`, products/device_units/sf_orders (TRUNCATE etc.), `sales_bill_no_seq` and the touch trigger functions.
  - The evidence bucket allows unlimited uploads per open handover.
  - `shop_income` was open to anon until 20261009040000. Confirm that migration is applied in production and check the table for tampering.

## Suggested order

1. One migration for findings 1–5. They are all in SQL, need no owner decision, and can follow PR #6.
2. An owner decision on finding 6, then one migration for the view changes.
3. A tidy-up migration for the low-priority grants and validation.
4. Then the safe test environment (Supabase branch or local stack, plus CI), so these migrations run somewhere other than production first.
